The story, year by year
The origin

I built the tool. Someone else had to use it.

I was an engineer, and I had built a tool I was proud of. My employer sent me to a cement plant to train the team on it. The technical folks picked it up in an hour. Then a truck driver snapped: he had spent months learning the old system, and now he had to start over, and he hated it.

I tried to reason with him. He would not hear it. That night it landed. What was effortless for me, the maker, was a wall for him, the user.

The switch

I stopped asking what the tech could do.

The real question was not what the technology could do. It was who is using it, and why. That flip changed my career. In 2009 I moved from engineering into design, and learned from mentors like Jared Spool and schools like Stanford.

I never dropped the engineering. I kept it, so that one day what I designed and what shipped could be the same thing.

The craft

Enterprise platforms, made clear.

Two decades on high-stakes enterprise platforms and systems, the tools people run real operations on. The instinct never changed: cut past what a stakeholder asks for to the need they cannot name, and build for that.

That is where the point of view formed. Clarity at density. Status first, detail last. Color only where it is a signal. Make a serious, complicated tool feel effortless.

Intuitive Surgical

The decision the data was hiding.

Surgical coordinators ordered the instruments a da Vinci robotic program runs on, from a portal that buried the one decision they needed under a spreadsheet. I found it and made the reorder signal a treemap you read in a glance.

Navigation collapsed from three and four levels to a single step.

Walmart Data Ventures

Six products. One foundation.

Six products, six teams, and about forty percent design debt. I audited the landscape, anchored a new system on Walmart's own Living Design, and proved it on one beta product before the rest followed.

Zero design debt, teams about thirty percent faster, and the only suite in the org on one foundation.

What's next

The job is still clarity.

Twenty-five years on, the instinct is the one the truck driver taught me: judge the tool through the person using it, never the other way around. Now I do it as a player-coach, and I ship what I design.

Open to Principal, Staff, and Director roles, and advisory work.
