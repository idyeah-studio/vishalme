IThe Spine

The real problem, not the symptom.

Stakeholders hand me a solution disguised as a problem. "We need a dashboard." "Add AI to it." My job is to find the real need, the one they cannot quite name, and make it clear. Whether the surface is a design system, an analytics platform, or an AI agent, the work is the same: find the real problem, not the symptom, and solve it.

IIIHow I Work

Four moves, every time.

01

Find the real problem

Research, then ask why until it stops. Watch what people actually do, not what they say they do. The brief is rarely the problem.

02

Make it clear

Systems thinking to reduce cognitive load. I design the order the eye travels: status first, detail last, so the answer arrives before the question.

03

Prove it

Prototype, test with real users, and tie the result to a business number. I build in code when it counts, so what ships is what I designed.

04

Lead it

Player-coach. Scale judgment across a team, not just pixels. The best mentoring makes you unnecessary, and the systems keep growing after you step back.

IVOrigin

I came to design from engineering.

Early on I watched someone struggle with a tool I had built and was proud of, and it landed hard: what was effortless for me, the maker, was a wall for them, the user. That flipped my question from what the technology can do to who is using it, and why. Twenty-five years later the job is the same: find the real problem, not the symptom, and fix it. I lead as a player-coach, and the systems I am proudest of kept growing after I stepped back.
