The through-line

Fix the problem, not the symptom.

I came to design from engineering. Early on I watched someone struggle with a tool I had built and was proud of, and it landed hard: what was effortless for me, the maker, was a wall for them, the user. That flipped my question from what the technology can do to who is using it, and why.

Twenty-five years later the job is the same. Stakeholders hand me a solution disguised as a problem, and I find the real need they cannot name, then make the complex, high-stakes system clear. I lead as a player-coach, and I build in code, so what ships is what I designed.
