02 clarity · the spine

The real problem is rarely the one you are handed.

Stakeholders hand me a solution disguised as a problem. "We need a dashboard." "Add AI to it." My job is to find the real need, the one they cannot quite name, and make it clear. Whether the surface is a design system, an analytics platform, or an AI agent, the work is the same: find the real problem, not the symptom, and solve it.

04 how I work

A method, not a mood.

01

Find the real problem

Research first. Ask why until it stops being a feature request. Watch what people actually do, not what they say they need.

02

Make it clear

Systems thinking over screens. Reduce cognitive load. Design the order the eye travels: status first, detail last.

03

Prove it

Prototype early. Test with real users. Tie the result to a business number, not a nicer-looking screen.

04

Lead it

Player-coach. Scale judgment across a team. The best mentoring makes you unnecessary.

And yes, I build in code, so what ships is what I designed.

05 about · origin

I came to design from engineering. Early on I watched someone struggle with a tool I had built and was proud of, and it landed hard: what was effortless for me, the maker, was a wall for them, the user. That flipped my question from what the technology can do to who is using it, and why.

Twenty-five years later the job is the same: find the real problem, not the symptom, and fix it. I lead as a player-coach, and the systems I am proudest of kept growing after I stepped back.
