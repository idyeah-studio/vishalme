01 // The scramble

96 failures. 4 tools. 2 hours. Zero answers.

It is 2am and the pipeline is red. Not one failure. A wall of them. Ninety-six rows, all shouting, none of them telling you which one actually matters.

So the ritual begins. Logs in one tab. Radar in another. Splunk in a third. Dashboards in a fourth. You copy an ID from here, paste it there, scroll, squint, and correlate it all in your head because nothing else will. This is the swivel chair, and every engineer on the team knows it by heart.

Two hours later you have twelve tabs, a cold coffee, and no clear answer. The failures are still red. You have confirmed that something is broken, which you already knew at row one.

02 // The reframe

What if the digging was already done?

Here is the question I kept circling. The engineer is not slow. The tools are not broken. The work of triage, gathering logs, correlating signals across services, spotting the pattern, is real work. And we were asking a human to do it by hand, at 2am, ninety-six times.

But that work is legible. It has a shape. It is exactly the kind of patient, cross-referencing labor a machine should carry so a human does not have to.

So I stopped designing a better place to look things up. I started designing a system that had already looked. The screen should not greet you with a wall and a search box. It should greet you with an answer, and let you audit it.

03 // The principle

Confidence before detail.

Every screen in Terran obeys one rule, and it is the spine of the whole product. Status first. Summary second. Evidence last.

You should know the state of a thing before you spend a single click on it. You should get the one-line summary before the stack trace. And the full evidence chain should be there the instant you want it, and invisible until you do.

This is not a layout preference. It is a hierarchy of trust. Most tools bury the answer under the evidence and make you assemble it. Terran leads with the answer and keeps the evidence one tap away. Same information. Opposite posture.

04 // Know before you click

The system tells you its state. No guessing.

Start at the smallest altitude: a single PR. Before Terran, a failing row looked exactly like every other row. Red, or maybe green, and nothing else. To learn anything you had to open it. Opening it was the tax.

In Terran, the row carries its state on its face. Being triaged, root cause in hand, or waiting on you, without clicking into anything. The list becomes scannable. Your attention goes to the rows that have earned it.

06 // Noise into signal

One cause. 17 failures explained.

Those ninety-six failures were never ninety-six problems. In the scenario I use to demo it, the agent traces seventeen of them to a single origin: a gRPC deadline timeout at IP 10.100. One cause. Seventeen failures explained.

This is the moment the noise collapses into signal. The wall stops being a wall. It becomes a short, honest list of actual root causes, each standing in front of the failures it accounts for. The count drops not because anything was hidden, but because the truth was finally grouped correctly.

And because Terran leads with confidence, you get that collapse first, as a headline, with the individual failures one tap underneath. Trust, but verify. The full chain is there, collapsed until you want it.

07 // Never a dead end

The diagnosis flows into action. No swivel chair.

A diagnosis that leaves you at a dead end is just a better-looking wall. So every conclusion in Terran is also a launchpad. From the root cause in front of you, create the Radar ticket, see the suggested code fix, open the Splunk logs, ask the agent a follow-up, or share the finding to Slack. In place, without leaving the answer.

This is where the swivel chair finally dies. The four tools did not go away. They came to the diagnosis, instead of you going to them. The context you used to assemble by hand at 2am is now assembled for you, and it stays attached to whatever you do next.

08 // Different altitude, same principle

One screen. One source of truth.

What works for one PR has to work for a whole team. The answer is not a different design. It is the same hierarchy of trust at a higher altitude. Status, summary, blockers, trajectory. And the trajectory tier is the one that changes behavior. Catch it on day one, not day five. The trend is the signal.

09 // How I built this

The shipped product is the designed product.

I did not design Terran in a canvas and hand it off. I designed it in the codebase. Stakeholders reviewed running prototypes, the real thing in the browser, with real interaction. I refined in that same artifact, then integrated it with the backend and the AI agent. There was no translation layer where the design goes to die.

Designed · in the codebase
The prototype stakeholders reviewed. Running, in the browser, real interaction.
Shipped · in production
The same artifact, integrated with the backend and the AI agent. Not a redraw.

The way I worked was to treat Claude Code as a configurable collaborator. I did not point it at a task and walk away. I directed it, and I switched the role I was directing it in as the work demanded. Each one a prompt away. Each one its own act of direction.
